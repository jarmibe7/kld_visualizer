What you are seeing

The target distribution P (blue) is a fixed mixture of Gaussians (default univariate). Q (green) is a single Gaussian approximation being optimized to minimize the KL divergence to P. The two plots show the optimization under reverse KL (left) and forward KL (right). Compare how the two KL objectives lead to different solutions. For univariate, use a learning rate of ~0.05. For multivariate, use a learning rate of ~0.003.

In the univariate case, the closed-form solution for the KL divergence between two Gaussians is used to compute the loss. In the multivariate case, the KL divergence is approximated by evaluating the distributions on a grid of points and computing the discrete KL divergence.

Reverse KL $D_{KL}(Q\|P)=\sum_x Q(x)\log\frac{Q(x)}{P(x)}$

Forward KL $D_{KL}(P\|Q)=\sum_x P(x)\log\frac{P(x)}{Q(x)}$
